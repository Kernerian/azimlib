Preserved early diagnostic (historical)

The original text and numerical results below are retained as historical evidence. They do not describe the current corpus or restore a release blocker that has been formally deferred. Decision and archived criteria.

The development domain is 1972–2100, airless geometric occultation with smooth limbs. Numerical domain checks are not evidence of uniform accuracy throughout that interval. Limb topography, polar motion, refraction, weather and safe observing advice are outside this model.

Implementation and separate conventions

  • Own DAF/SPK type 2/3 reader, IEEE little/big endian, explicit local file and SHA-256, maximum 64 MiB, bounded descriptors/degrees/center chains, geometric barycentric ICRF/J2000 states in metres and metres/second at TDB seconds.
  • Explicit TT and UT1 coordinates. Leading periodic TT–TDB conversion, iterative light time, geocentric aberration, IAU-1976 precession, four dominant nutation terms and sidereal rotation. No inferred UTC/UT1 equivalence.
  • Physical disks and near-side shadow-axis intersection with WGS84; spherical finite-source umbra/penumbra/antumbra remain distinct from visual exaggeration.
  • Physical mean lunar radius is the default. The explicitly named NASA bulletin internal/external lunar-limb presets use k=0.272281 / k=0.2725076 respectively; selecting one does not silently change the Sun radius or reproduce every historical catalog convention. Current ground comparison uses physical mean disks and records the convention mismatch as unresolved, rather than fitting constants to the desired result.
  • Lunar contacts separately declare airless mean or NASA Danjon effective mean shadow. The latter uses the published 1.01 Earth-radius convention; it is not a physical atmosphere model or the exact projected Earth ellipsoid.
  • Contact searches are bounded bracket/bisection searches. Grazing/double roots are not covered and cannot be represented as validated missed events.

The short orientation reduction has no established global accuracy bound. accuracy_validated remains false. Kernel quality alone cannot certify the reductions, apparent directions, Earth orientation or eclipse geometry.

Independent evidence and current errors

Summary and source hashes record maxima, coverage and pass/fail status. Full per-coordinate errors are generated externally. All 302 timed central coordinates are compared: 97 in 2017, 107 in 2023, 98 in 2024. The catalog supplies 601 boundary coordinates; three northern coordinates are explicitly absent near the terrestrial limb. Those rows keep their valid center/southern coordinates and are never silently dropped.

There are 29 failing 2023 boundary coordinates. Boundary comparison minimizes the internal-contact margin in a fixed ±15-minute window, then solves latitude at the published longitude. Meridian distance is a conservative comparison to the swept envelope, not a conversion of angular residual into ground distance. Searching the full fixed ±1-degree meridian interval prevents two nearby roots from being lost when both interval ends are outside a narrow track.

Lunar P1/U1/U2/U3/U4/P4 contacts: maximum absolute error 1.917 s for 2022-11-08 and 3.032 s for 2025-03-14, both within 60 s using the declared Danjon convention. The two clear controls also pass. These sampled successes do not establish a global accuracy bound.

Solar internal durations agree within 5.691 s across the 302 locations, but duration agreement is not validation of absolute C1/C2/C3/C4 times. Supplemental Carbondale/Albuquerque/Dallas cases and a 30 s target were fixed before attempted USNO acquisition. The official API was inaccessible during this run; no substitute contact times or guessed offsets were inserted. The frozen supplemental cases have SHA-256 78db9334c988cbd2ce3ed321e92d6e59490b7afa7724c21754cefee277a2cc4f; their 30 s target is unchanged. A later acquisition must record complete responses, query URL, retrieval date, SHA-256, native timescale and all events before model comparison, preserving a failed or missing event in the report.

The independent jplephem 2.24 reader comparison at ten fixed epochs gives maximum position disagreement 0.000061629 m and velocity disagreement 1.48e-11 m/s. It validates our SPK evaluation against another reader of the same DE440s data, not the physical ephemeris or Earth-orientation model. NASA catalogs use their separately computed eclipse predictions.

Reproduction and remaining work

Place the six original references and frozen-contract.json in a directory outside the checkout. URLs, byte counts and SHA-256 are fixed in the contract. There is no automatic fetch or runtime fallback:

python -I tools/validate_eclipse_contract.py \
  --reference-dir EXTERNAL_REFERENCES --output EXTERNAL_REPORT.json

The auditor verifies all hashes, rejects incomplete/duplicate timed rows and returns 3 for the unpassed scientific gate. Its report separates numerical execution from scientific acceptance; successful unit tests cannot override it. Raw references and full coordinate comparisons are not included in packages.

Required next work: establish an independently supported Earth-orientation and apparent-state error budget; resolve the 2023 trajectory and catalog disk/limb conventions without fitting; acquire and compare all fixed absolute solar contacts; cover grazing events; then repeat every frozen case. Only after this contract is satisfied can precise event visualization conclude 13.06/13.07.
