Original palette

AZIM10 in src/azimlib/cycles.py is a new ten-color sequence selected for Azimlib. The default and dark-background cycles use this sequence or its own lightened variant. TAB10 remains a compatibility alias for AZIM10; it does not contain the Tableau/D3/Matplotlib Category10 sequence. Individual color values are not represented as globally unique or exclusively owned. The historical series oracle retains default-cycle roles rather than the retired ten RGB values; tests materialize those roles with AZIM10.

Separately installed dependencies

No external Python packages are embedded in the Azimlib wheel. Optional extras install Pillow (MIT-CMU), aggdraw (permissive PIL-style terms including AGG), NumPy (BSD and additional bundled notices), and Numba/llvmlite (BSD plus LLVM and other component notices). Python and Tcl/Tk retain their own terms. Redistributing a combined application requires auditing and preserving the licenses of the actual dependency binaries, including Pillow codecs, NumPy's BLAS/LAPACK and any GCC runtime exception. See docs/dependencies.md.

Matplotlib, fontTools, cycler, contourpy, Qt/PySide and Playwright are development or comparison tools, not the cartographic backend. Their code/assets are not vendored into the package. Using them does not make their licenses the license of Azimlib's original implementation. Distributing those tools is a separate licensing task.

External IBGE benchmark inputs are not shipped. Their specific redistribution terms have not been established here; they must be checked before bundling any such dataset. Numerical benchmark records do not grant a dataset license.

Generated gallery illustrations use synthetic fields/routes and the separately credited geographic/font resources above. Reference-comparison previews are rendered with development tools and retain their producer metadata. They are not vendored implementations or toolbar artwork. The wheel contains no gallery; see docs/publication-readiness.md for the local source ZIP and preview scope.

Optional complex typography (0.4 development)

The original text_engine.py compositor and _unicode_icu.py C-API binding use separately installed generic text engines, not Matplotlib implementations: uharfbuzz (Apache-2.0), HarfBuzz (permissive upstream notices), fontTools (MIT), aggdraw (its existing PIL/AGG notices) and the operating system's ICU. No engine binary, source, generated Unicode table or third-party Python binding is vendored. The text extra does not introduce python-bidi or LGPL bidi code. _bidi.py is an original implementation of the normative UAX #9 rules, using ICU character properties rather than ICU's bidi resolver. No bidi implementation was copied, translated or ported from another project.

Full reference notices are in licenses/UHarfBuzz.txt, licenses/HarfBuzz.txt, licenses/FontTools.txt and licenses/ICU.txt; origins/versions/SHA-256 are in docs/text-dependencies-0.4.json. ICU's notice is a pinned upstream reference, not a declaration that all installed ICU releases have identical terms. When redistributing engine binaries, preserve the notices for the actual resolved version, including transitive notices supplied by its distributor.

Optional Unicode 15.1 conformance datasets are acquired separately, with pinned sizes/hashes by tools/validate_unicode_text.py. They are not in wheel/sdist. Their Unicode copyrights remain in the unmodified files; the acquisition also preserves the full official permission notice in licenses/Unicode-Data.txt. That notice is not replaced by Azimlib's BSD license. The standard's algorithmic rules inform the original resolver; no prose or reference implementation from the standard is incorporated into it.

Optional Noto Devanagari and Noto CJK validation fonts retain OFL-1.1 and their upstream copyrights, including reserved-name conditions where present. Solar model definitions and reference-only validation (0.4 development)

solar.py, solar_artists.py and validate_solar.py are original Azimlib code. Mathematical model definitions are credited to the USNO approximate solar equations; the optional atmospheric approximation is described by NOAA. Their code, prose, binaries and datasets have not been incorporated. Sources, validity and model limitations are recorded in the solar guide. JPL Horizons is an independently queried validation reference with its own terms; no raw responses, numerical ephemeris tables, kernels or JPL code are packaged or relicensed under BSD. Only original analysis, query identities and input hashes appear in development evidence. These distinctions do not change the license of any existing bundled third-party material.

Lunar model definitions and reference-only validation (0.4 development)

lunar.py, lunar_artists.py and the lunar validation tools are independently written Azimlib code. The mathematical short-series definitions are credited to the Almanac model reviewed in David G. Simpson's NASA GSFC paper, equations 1–4, pages 176–177. NTRS record 19990040527 identifies the work as US government material permitting public use. Only the model's numerical definitions are expressed in code; no paper text, third-party implementation or ephemeris kernel is bundled or claimed as exclusive property. The Moon's mean radius is a numerical physical fact credited to the NASA factsheet. Sources, hashes, frames and limitations are documented in docs/lunar-model-0.4.json and the lunar guide. JPL Horizons is validation-only, under its own terms: no raw numerical reference tables/responses are distributed or relicensed. Original error statistics and query hashes are retained.

Physical light and finite-source geometry (0.4 development)

lighting.py, eclipse.py, eclipse_artists.py and their validation/examples are independently written Azimlib implementations of generic geometric ideas. The nominal Sun radius is a numerical definition credited to IAU 2015 B3 (source); Earth/Moon constants retain the credits recorded in docs/lighting-model-0.4.json and docs/lunar-model-0.4.json. No third-party ray tracer, eclipse implementation or ephemeris kernel is included. Fred Espenak's NASA/GSFC eclipse path table is an independent reference only: raw HTML, numerical tables, text and images are not distributed. Original diagnostic statistics and input hashes do not claim ownership of those sources. Precise real-event prediction is unsupported; this does not change the license of any previously bundled third-party material.

Experimental eclipse ephemeris validation (0.4 development)

_spk.py is an independently written, bounded reader/evaluator based on the public NAIF DAF/SPK format specifications. No SPICE, SOFA, ERFA or jplephem implementation is included. JPL DE440s remains externally supplied reference data under its own terms; the kernel is neither bundled nor relicensed here. The separate comparison with jplephem 2.24 is development-only, not a runtime dependency or an independent validation of the underlying dynamical model.

_eclipse_ephemeris.py contains original experimental reductions and geometry. Conventional IAU-1976 precession parameters, truncated nutation, light time, aberration and Chebyshev recurrence are mathematical/physical definitions; their use does not establish an exclusive right over those definitions. It is not a complete IAU/IERS Earth-orientation implementation.

Solar path and lunar contact references are credited as: Eclipse Predictions by Fred Espenak, NASA's GSFC. The validation tool includes twelve numeric lunar contact expectations; original PDFs/HTML tables, prose, artwork and ephemeris kernels are not shipped. NASA's published lunar-limb and Danjon effective-shadow conventions retain their attribution and are separate from the physical mean-radius defaults. Sources, input hashes and the unpassed scientific accuracy criteria are in the experimental eclipse guide.
